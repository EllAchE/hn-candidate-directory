How profiles get here

Public posts, made searchable.

Listings are read from Hacker News “Who wants to be hired?” threads and normalized into companies, universities, skills, dates, and locations. Each card links back to the comment it came from.

A listing compiled from a Hacker News comment is published without being asked for, so it can be taken down without a token or an account: open the profile and choose “This is me — remove my listing”. Removal is immediate, and the comment is not collected again.

A resume, LinkedIn profile, or source text you submit yourself works the other way around. It becomes a private draft that stays out of search until it is reviewed and explicitly approved, and the token issued at submission can update or remove it later.

Pilot feedback

Tell us what is wrong or missing

This directory is an early pilot. Wrong data, a listing that should not be here, a search that missed something, or an idea — all of it is read.

Feedback is stored privately for the operator; it is never published to the directory. To take your own listing down, use “Update or remove your listing” instead — that is immediate.
Private candidate submission

Review before you publish

Choose one source. A background job extracts structured fields into a private draft. Saving edits stays private; publication requires a separate, explicit approval.

Submission source

Only public HTTPS linkedin.com/in/… profiles are accepted. Redirect, network, page, time, and byte controls run before a draft is stored.

This first upload format accepts one UTF-8 .txt file up to 100,000 bytes with a simple ASCII filename. PDF, Word, HTML, archive, executable, and binary files are rejected. The private staging copy is deleted after successful extraction, with a short lifecycle as backstop.

Your review token stays in this tab. Pending and review-ready drafts are excluded from the public candidate endpoint.
